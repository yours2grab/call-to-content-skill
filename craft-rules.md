# Craft Rules: LinkedIn Post Writing

Apply every rule to every draft. These separate a post that stops the scroll from a post that gets skimmed and scrolled past.

---

## Rule 0 (absolute): Never use the em dash

Never put an em dash (`—`) in a draft. It reads as AI-generated. Use a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or rephrase. A regular hyphen (`-`) is fine. This overrides every other rule.

---

## Batch rule (applies whenever drafting more than one post at once)

**No two drafts in the same batch share a core line or insight.** Rule 1 below ("every line = new information") applies within a single draft, this is the same rule applied across the whole batch. Before presenting any batch, compare every draft against every other draft: if two drafts land on the same takeaway, even worded differently, rewrite one of them around a different angle from the mined list. Each draft must stand alone as a distinct idea a reader could see twice without noticing overlap.

## The 10 rules

1. **Every line = new information.** Same idea in different words = delete the duplicate.
2. **Arrows (→) are reader takeaways.** Not story narration. Not a reformatted sentence.
3. **One personality moment per post, minimum.** A specific aside, a real reaction, a moment only this person would have. If the post could've been written by anyone, it's wrong.
4. **Talk TO the reader.** 50%+ of the post should use "you" or address them directly.
5. **Hook max 8 words.**
6. **120-300 words preferred. 400 hard max.**
7. **No banned words:** actually, boundless, impeccable, leverage, synergy, unlock, empower, revolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, landscape, dive in, deep dive, robust, seamless, holistic, paradigm, optimize, scalable, innovative, navigate, elevate, utilize, streamline, cutting-edge.
8. **End with a question or a soft CTA.** Never "book a call," never fake scarcity ("only 3 spots left").
9. **Compression.** If you can say it in fewer words, do it. Three words beats a paragraph.
10. **Don't fabricate.** If it isn't in the transcript, it didn't happen. No invented quotes, numbers, outcomes, or audiences.

---

## Instant fails

- Bold text
- Em dashes or horizontal dashes used as punctuation
- ALL CAPS
- Multiple emojis
- Stacked negatives ("No X. No Y. No Z.")
- "In today's fast-paced world" or any variant
- "Here's the thing" / "Let's dive in" / "What if I told you"

---

## Every draft: why, then steps

This is the shape of every draft this skill produces, not just ones tagged "how-to."

1. Hook (≤8 words)
2. Why this matters, 2-4 sentences, the opinion or context. Wherever the angle's story or contrarian take lives, it lives here, kept short.
3. The steps. Numbered or arrow-led (→). 3-6 real actions, not phases or morals. This is the dominant part of the post, the reason someone screenshots it.
4. A question or soft CTA to close.

A step earns its place by being something a reader could literally go do. "Nurture your list" is not a step. "Send 3 real emails a week, not a newsletter blast" is. If an angle can't produce real steps, it wasn't strong enough to draft in the first place, go back to the mined list and pick a different one.

## Format (LinkedIn-native, not generic copywriting)

- Hook: one line, max 8 words, no period at the end
- 1-2 sentences per paragraph
- Heavy white space, short paragraphs
- The first 2-3 lines matter most, everything else is below the "see more" fold
- One idea per post. Don't try to fit two lessons in one draft
- Link (if any) goes in the first comment, never in the post body
- 0-2 real questions max, not rhetorical filler
- No hashtag stuffing. Zero, or one that's genuinely load-bearing

---

## Specificity

- **Odd numbers beat round numbers.** "17 client calls" beats "20 client calls." Round numbers read as made up.
- **Show a scene, don't state a claim.** "She said 'I almost gave up' on minute 40 of the call" beats "the client was frustrated."
- **Dialogue beats paraphrase.** Use the transcript's real words when they're strong. Direct quotes carry more than a summary of what someone meant.
- **Strong verbs over adjectives.** Cut the adjective, find the verb doing the work.

---

## Sentence rhythm

Vary length constantly. Short. Medium. One longer sentence that carries an idea through a full breath. Then a snap back.

Like that.

Cut any sentence that doesn't survive the removal test: take it out, does the meaning survive? Then it was filler.

---

## The test that decides everything

Read the draft aloud. If you stumble, rewrite the line. If it sounds like a real person telling a friend something that actually happened, ship it. If it sounds like a LinkedIn "thought leader" post, start over.

Every draft this skill produces must trace back to something specific that was actually said on the call. That's the whole point: not a generic post about a topic, a real moment turned into a post.
