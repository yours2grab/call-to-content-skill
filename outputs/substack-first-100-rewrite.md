## The P.R.A.I.S.E. Framework

P - Pay attention (like)
R - Respond thoughtfully (comment on notes)
A - Appreciate deeply (comment on articles)
I - Individual outreach (DM)
S - Strengthen trust (email)
E - Expand the relationship (offer)

Most people treat Substack engagement as a random like and comment session. The problem with that is you can't measure your effort.

The first four letters are the soul of Substack. Likes, comments and DMs. That's what we're unpacking here, because that's what gets you to 100. The last two, email and offer, are what you do after. That's a whole article on its own and I'll write it.

I call the first four the four layers of relationship building.

Every layer costs you more effort than the one before it. And that is the whole point. The more effort it costs you, the more it is worth to the person receiving it.

Let me show you.

---

### P - Pay attention (like)

The lowest form of praise is a like.

I know creators who like hundreds of posts without reading them. And you feel it when it happens to you. Because a like is easy to give and very easy to receive.

When you get a like, mostly you never hear from that person again. And be honest with yourself. How many times have you interacted with somebody who liked you?

Now this does not mean you shouldn't like a post. It means you treat the like as a starting point for building a relationship. Not a one off action.

So this is what I do. I start with the notes of my own subscribers. It keeps me front of mind with the people who already said yes to me.

Then I go to people who are not connected to me yet. Cold people.

The like has one job. It gets you noticed. That's all it does. Don't ask it to do more.

---

### R - Respond thoughtfully (comment on notes)

A comment on a note is a lot more commitment.

I don't need to explain that notes are the soul of Substack. There are hundreds of articles written about notes.

I'm more interested in the psychological impact of a comment.

Think about this for a second. That person spent years getting the knowledge. Substack is filled with people with a ton of experience. Including you. And they spent time to write that note.

So your job is simple. Read it. Then leave a thoughtful comment.

This is not a signal to start sending random comments. "Great note" does nothing. You make waves by actually reading the note and adding your own take on it.

That's what gets you seen by the writer. But also by the audience of that writer.

I call that the network effect.

A few thoughtful comments can bring you in front of a completely new audience. And the writer notices you too. This person will check your profile, read your articles, and restack your posts.

Remember the law of reciprocity. It all starts with a few thoughtful comments.

### A - Appreciate deeply (comment on articles)

Why does a comment on an article beat every other engagement effort?

If a like is low effort, and a comment on a note is a little bit more effort, then a comment on an article is the highest effort and the highest praise you can give a Substack writer.

Why?

Because you know exactly what it costs. You spent hours thinking about your article. You put your heart and soul into writing it. Then you spent a couple of days editing it, making it perfect, uploading pictures. It is a big thing for you to finally push publish.

So when a person takes the time to read the whole article, gives you praise for it, and adds their own take on it, that is the highest form of reciprocity you can give.

And people love that.

This is where most of my subscribers came from. Not from likes. From article comments.

But I have to be honest with you. It's also the slowest layer. You can only read so many articles in a day. Which brings me to the fourth one.

---

### I - Individual outreach (DM)

The DM is a sacred place. Never abuse the space.

What amazes me on Substack is that I hardly get pitches in my DMs. When you live on LinkedIn for a long time, you know that pitches in DMs are normal.

Seth Godin uses a concept called permission based marketing. When somebody has their DMs open, that does not mean you can walk in and start selling.

You must earn the right.

And this is where people get confused, so let me be very clear.

A cold DM is not the problem. A cold pitch is the problem.

The ghostwriter who landed in my LinkedIn inbox earned nothing. He asked for money in message one. That's the slimey version.

But a DM to somebody whose notes you liked, whose note you commented on, and whose article you gave a real comment? That person is not cold anymore. You showed up three times. They know your name.

That is what earning the right means.

And when you start getting replies on your comments, that's your signal to move to the DM.

I have two guiding principles when I send one.

Start with a pattern interrupt.
Praise them for something.

That's it.

I start with a weird statement. "Sorry to drop in cold in your inbox." In fact this could have been anything, but in this case I choose the drop in cold approach, because it's honest.

Then I praise them for something specific. People love it when you acknowledge their work.

The goal of that first DM is not a sale. It's a conversation.

Because once you have a conversation, it can lead to so many things:

→ A free subscriber
→ A paid subscriber
→ A Substack live
→ A guest post
→ A friendship

Both of my paid subscribers came out of a DM conversation. Not from a post. Not from a like.

So the article comments brought me the subscribers, and the DMs turned them into paid ones. That's why I spend most of my time in the DMs.

But only after the first three layers. Never before them.

## Get out of your echo chamber

Now here is the trap.

Substack shows you the people you are already connected with. Your followers and your subscribers. And it's comfortable in there. Everybody knows your name.

But if you only engage inside that circle, you are talking to the same room every day. No new people, no growth.

And you can't ignore that room either. Those are the people who already said yes to you, and they will go quiet if you do.

So I split it. Half of my engagement goes to my followers and subscribers. The other half goes to people who never heard of me.

The warm half keeps you. The cold half grows you.

That one rule is the difference between being busy and growing.

## The daily system

Pick one slot in your day and keep it. Same time, every day. I do mine in the morning with coffee.

It takes me 45 to 60 minutes. I'm not going to sell you a 10 minute version, because it doesn't exist. But it's the same hour every day and I don't think about it anymore.

**1. Likes (5 minutes)**

Go through your notes feed and like 20 to 30 notes from your subscribers and followers. This number is high because you can do this super fast.

Then go to the explore tab, find your niche, and like 20 to 30 notes from people you are not connected to yet.

That's your warm half and your cold half, done in five minutes.

**2. Comments on notes (15 minutes)**

5 thoughtful comments on notes from people in your network.

5 more in the explore section, on new people.

Read the note first. Every time. If you can't add anything, move on to the next one.

**3. Comments on articles (20 minutes)**

This is the slow one, and it's the one that pays.

2 articles from your feed, 2 from people you don't know yet.

Read the whole thing. Then write a paragraph with your own insight.

Avoid: "nice article." That's a like with extra steps.

**4. DMs (20 minutes)**

This is where I spend most of my time.

Always start with answering the incoming ones. It's a great way to warm up.

Then 5 DMs to your warm network. And 5 to cold people you already liked and commented on this week. Never to somebody you touched for the first time today.

Write like you chat with a friend. Keep it short and simple. Don't write long paragraphs. Think of a DM as a WhatsApp chat with a buddy and you'll do fine.

That's 10 DMs, 4 article comments, 10 note comments and around 50 likes.

Do it for 18 days and tell me in the comments what happened.

## One more thing: the subscriber chat

Before I sign off I want to give you the one that skyrocketed my growth.

Participate in the chats of big publications.

Pick 2 or 3 big ones and be active in there. Use the framework: like and comment on the posts inside the chat.

But this is where it gets powerful.

After you like or comment in the chat, go to that person's profile. 5 likes and one real comment on their notes is a great start.

Mostly they will like or comment back. And if the match is good, they subscribe.

Why does it work so well? Because the chat is a warm room. You are not a stranger dropping in cold, you are somebody they just talked to. The profile visit closes the loop.

I got a lot of subscribers with this method, and so will you.
